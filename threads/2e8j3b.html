Save Jizzle! The Freudian Psychology Theory

▲ 24 ptsu/lbraudAug 22, 201425 comments at captureOpen original on Reddit ↗
Documented under:Jungian / Archetypal AnalysisJungian Archetype Frame Reading

Feel free to skip down to "what if," if you are already on board with Trevor, Michael, and Franklin being the id, ego, and superego.

It is obvious to anyone with some background in psychology that the three playable characters are personifications of Freud's stuctural model of the psyche. A quick search of this sub for Freud and superego show that it may not be obvious to all chiliad hunters, so I'll provide some background before dropping the greatest, absolutely truest, never thought of before theoryjk on you.

Freud was a coke-head that slept with his wife's sister. He also developed a theory of mind where all of our impulses were driven by the desire to receive pleasure or avoid pain. To facilitate this, we have the id, the part of our personality that only wants its desires met.

From Psychology 101:

According to Freud, we are born with our Id. The id is an important part of our personality because as newborns, it allows us to get our basic needs met. Freud believed that the id is based on our pleasure principle. In other words, the id wants whatever feels good at the time, with no consideration for the reality of the situation. When a child is hungry, the id wants food, and therefore the child cries. When the child needs to be changed, the id cries. When the child is uncomfortable, in pain, too hot, too cold, or just wants attention, the id speaks up until his or her needs are met.

The id doesn't care about reality, about the needs of anyone else, only its own satisfaction. If you think about it, babies are not real considerate of their parents' wishes. They have no care for time, whether their parents are sleeping, relaxing, eating dinner, or bathing. When the id wants something, nothing else is important.

This is Trevor. He bangs a meth head and turns around and stomps her husband's brains out. Then wipes out his gang, and then their competition. He gets bored, he goes down to the docks and steals -he doesn't even know what- from a para-military force. He's unchecked impulse personified.

As the baby gets a little older and experiences the world around them, it starts to take the long view. Demanding immediate gratification isn't the best way to get what it wants. This naturally gives rise to the second part of the personality called the ego. The id sees a toy he wants that another child is playing with, but the ego puts him in check because he doesn't want to get fussed at, get bitten, or hurt his friend's feelings. The impulse still needs to be satisfied however, so the ego may find a different toy or steal the child's when he's not looking.

The ego is Michael. He isn't really interested in a score if it isn't planned out, but he's got no problem using those around him to get what he needs.

Finally the superego develops. It's the part of our personality that differentiates between right and wrong. This is clearly Franklin, who urges Michael to outrun Madrazo's men, when Michael wants him to "use the gun, that's what it's for." Franklin also questions Lamar, "You crazy mother fucker. Why'd we have to pop all those people?"

So clearly, our three characters are literary symbols for a popular psychological theory. Writers do this all the time. Some writers put so much symbology in their stories they forget to put a story in their story - like Ulysses.

Now what if, if, our three characters aren't literary symbols for the subconscious, but are actually literal aspects of the subconscious of the story's real protagonist who we barely know?

The psych report at the end of the game isn't for you, the player, but the real lead character that goes to therapy. Michael's sessions, and the entire "90's action movie" drama that plays out is just a hyperstylized, distorted version of a mundane reality.

That's right. We're going all Fight Club/Shutter Island/Inception up in this mother fucker. A plot twist that is much more worthy of R* than the three hollow endings we've found so far.

One minor character in GTA V would imagine this type of drama. One weened on violent video games, rap music, an over-bearing father and fueled by adolescent testosterone. Yep, Jimmy De Santa. Jimmy continuously verbalizes therapy talk (back of the car after the boat chase in Father/Son, on the way to buy drugs) and Jimmy is also the only character that can "Hang Out" with all three of the playable characters.

"So what?" you say. What could we do that? If we look back to Freud's structural model, we can find a definition of healthy and possibly use that to make Jimmy healthy.

Again from Psychology 101:

In a healthy person, according to Freud, the ego is the strongest so that it can satisfy the needs of the id, not upset the superego, and still take into consideration the reality of every situation. Not an easy job by any means, but if the id gets too strong, impulses and self gratification take over the person's life. If the superego becomes to strong, the person would be driven by rigid morals, would be judgmental and unbending in his or her interactions with the world.

So balancing the behaviors of the characters is one possibility. And that is as far as I've gotten with this theory.
