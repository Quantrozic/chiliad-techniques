Could "moral" decisions influence the output of the Easter eggs?

▲ 5 ptsu/badillinOct 2, 201338 comments at captureOpen original on Reddit ↗
Documented under:Moral Choice & Playstyle Branching Analysis

I read this theory about the game being a journey, and that certain apparently unconsequential choices you make in the game influence the outcome (read this from this annoying user that keeps "yelling" that people are ripping off his theory or something)...

Anyways, i thought he might be up to something... (LONG Shot, but still)

As far as i can tell, the game TELLS YOU you have an option to kill or let go only 2 NPCs, the Psychoatrist and Dreyfuss (the one you get from getting all the letter fragments), im at 82% and havent done everything so there might be someone else.

I killed both obviously... but i wonder if letting them go would trigger something later (dead men give out no missions)...

i dont really know how any of this two characters would have any influence on the UFOs or Jetpack, but still i found it curious that you can actually LET GO a confessed torturer/murderer (on a extremely short "side mission" that lasts 3 minutes after +2hrs of finding 50 scraps!) and you can also let the psychiatrist get rich telling your secrets/life to everyone.

Why would the game give you this choices??? you dont get a choice to let go other characters that could as easily been left behind... whats the difference between this two? is there a REAL difference depending on what you choose?

Other than that (where the game specifically tells you, you can kill or let them go) you dont have a lot of moral decisions where you can actively choose to do the right thing (mission fails if you let them go).

I can only think of a few other "moral" choices during gameplay: did you take people to be eaten by the cult? and did you deliver Packie safely? did you let the guy you tortured go, or did you killed him just after he thought he was home free?...

This are the only choices i can think of where the NPC is "important" enough. and the Alturist camp, well you can finish the game and never deliver anyone... and they... might eat each other? who knows?

Im not really sure this fits with all the Chiliad Mystery theme, because its so unrelated to anything the Chiliad Mystery is about, but still...
