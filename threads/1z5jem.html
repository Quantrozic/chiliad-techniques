How I look at the Morality of our mains, and Karma vs. Morality

▲ 0 ptsu/TriggaMike403Feb 28, 201418 comments at captureOpen original on Reddit ↗
Documented under:Karma / Pacifist Playthrough ProtocolMoral Choice & Playstyle Branching Analysis

I really don't agree with how the characters are viewed in the sense of karma and karmic play throughs. From what I've gathered a lot of people tend to think:

  • Franklin - Good
  • Michael - Neutral
  • Trevor - Bad

Karma is a principle that actions and intentions affect a persons future.

Morality is the differentiation of intentions, decisions, and actions between those that are good and those that are bad.

To those who don't understand, Karma is an application of Morality. For a good majority of this post, I will talk about the Morality of our characters, since in talking about that we can determine if they are good or bad. Afterwards I will discuss separating Karma and Morality.

-----Michael-----

Michael is looked at sometimes as moral neutral, and sometimes even morally good. To support my case against this I use the following:

  • Trying to turn in his supposed friend and criminal counter part Trevor, getting his other friend Brad killed in the process, pretending to die, and accepting money in return for helping promote Dave Norton.
  • Hiding in the back of a car and forcing a random black guy who happened to break into his house to crash said car into the dealership where he works at gun point.
  • Pulling a house down a hill after catching his wife cheating on him. In fact his wife is the instigator judging from her track record, revealed later, and yet Michael never confronts her, instead confronting the men that she chooses to sleep with. He also busts a lady's iFruit laptop over Fabiens head later in the game
  • Multiple heists, frauds, and murders involving his former accomplice that he tried to have killed, Trevor, and a supposed 'good kid', Franklin
  • The mission at the Kortz Center where you have to kill at least 50 members of IAA, FIB, and Merry Weather.

In conclusion, Michael's general discourse, treatment of his family, and focus on his own personal gain are tell tale about his morality. He follows no real moral compass and goes through life simply doing as he pleases. He is also incredibly self centred, and would do just about anything to obtain what he feels he needs. His only limit is not killing his family, or Franklin, who he thinks of as the son he wished he had.

-----Franklin-----

Considered by some to be the most moral, given more freedom to decide by the player his karmic path. But is this really the case? When Michael first confronts him in Jimmy's car about stealing it, he replies that he is just doing a job for money, and he doesn't think about it beyond that. Any moral person questions where their income comes from. In his opinion Simeon could be using the cars to fuel a fire that he throws kittens into and he wouldn't care as long as it pays. A few more bulleted examples:

  • Mission with Lamar where they meet Stretch at the Bottle Depot. Countless gangsters dead
  • Grove Street mission where you bring Trevor and Lamar and end up wasting even more gangsters
  • Lumber Mill mission involving all three. Franklin instigates this to save Lamar.
  • Franklin's general opinion and treatment of his Auntie. Yes she is nutso at times, but she isn't hurting anyone with it. Does that justify how he talks about her and to her?

In conclusion Franklin's moral compass is also absent. His decisions are made in the heat of the moment, and he just prefers not to think about it. This is not morality, it is justifying the means through the ends. He is not nearly as self centred as Michael however, as shown through constant concern about Lamar, and Tonia.

Note: Killing gangsters is in no way moral. Killing anyone is immoral.

-----Trevor-----

Instead of pointing out the ways Trevor is immoral, I am going to point out the ways in which he actually has more of a moral compass than both Michael and Franklin.

  • Patricia. He did not kill her, in fact he treated her with more kindness and dignity than any other character who interacts with her.
  • He admits to Michael and Franklin that he is not a moral person. He is self aware. This is a step in the right direction.
  • Instead of being completely consumed with his own self interest, such as the cases of Michael, he actually has a group of friends that he supports and loves in his own weird way. When you do the Merry Weathef heist he isn't the one that he says will get rich, he refers to Ron, Wade, and himself getting rich.
  • Although he does not abide by it, he talks on multiple occasions of morality, and actual grasps the concept.
  • In general, he sticks up for his friends

In conclusion, Trevor is by far not a moral gem, but of the three protagonists he is the most self aware, actually has some form of moral compass, and actually makes effort to follow that. In my opinion, he is the most morally capable of the three.

-----Final Conclusion on Our Protagonists-----

In a game wrought with immorality, none of our protagonists shine. I have only used a few examples for each to limit the size of this post. But Morality does not exist in a vacuum. By that I mean none of our characters are considered good by any standard, but by definition of having morals, and at least following them, our shining star is none other than Trevor Phillips.

-----Karma vs. Morality-----

I understand why people tend to think that we should play this game in a good way, because of recurring themes of Karma. If you actually separate what Karma is from Morality, then you realize that it could also mean that your choices affect your future, and I think we all can agree this game makes that horribly apparent. Heist choices effect out game world, the end choice of killing a controllable player, and many other more minor choices of whether or not to kill certain not exactly main characters. Choosing to take or not take certain randoms to the altruists can permanently affect things such as Golf, and returning a stolen bike can land you $100,000 in company stock early on.

I am not judging anyone for their choices on attempting to have a clean play through. I'm all for a challenge, and this seems incredibly challenging. I am skeptical about it leading to a successful resolution however, but we will see in time.
