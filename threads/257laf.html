tl;dr at the end!

first of all:

i went back to basics, looking at the mural, thinking about its origins - i know we already had that, but i wanted to do a sober formal analysis (no tinfoil included) which i haven't seen done like this before:

analysis:

a simple comparison of the style of lines and strokes that constitute the chilliad mural with other wall paintings in the game shows that, as far as i know, the exact same characteristic style of lines is nowhere else to be found in the game (please correct me if i am wrong but read a little bit further first!). the strokes we see in the mural obviously seem rough and scratchy, which is an indication for that something rudimentary as mineralic based pigments like charcoal or earthcolours were used to make it. a closer look at the quality of the strokes as seen in the chilliad mural signifies that its not painted with a graffiti can nor with a brush or some modern marker-pen. also keep in mind that rockstar shows us many times in the game that they are capable of imitating any (!) style of painting, drawing and texture flawlessly as we see in many other painted places and walls everywhere in the game! therefore the chilliad mural's specific painting style must be considered fully intentional. ill come back to this in a sec.

like the paintings found at the altruist and hippie camp and some graffitis spread all over san andreas (like the "x"-s and various written messages) the style of other wallpaintings we come across in the game is in many cases a close resemblance of the style of the chiliad mural, but its never the exact same "brush"-work! nevertheless, the lack of formal analysis and the wild guessings on the origins of the chilliad mural have led chilliad-mystery-hunters to jump to rather random conclusions like for example that the altruists painted the mural, the hippies did it, epsilon did it, trevor did it…etc.. the rather similar style of the paintings and especially the symbols (eye/ sun/ ufo/ phi…) of the cults i named above and the chilliad mural truly suggest a connection between them - and respectively with the mystery - but they may share only the same (symbolic) meaning not the same author! according to my observation it seems like the original style or concept based on the pictorial language of the chilliad mural – which, according to my own theory that i am going to explain in the following, is much older than all the other cults in the game – only transcended into the symbolic realm of the present cults in the world of gta5. but ill come back to this as well.

chumash and tongva tribes:

a quick look at the map of gta5, in the west of los santos county we see some places with names like "chumash" and "tongva". those are two big native american tribes located in california – for those who didn't know (me included). their story goes way back, some more then 10.000 years. as gta 5 is a very, very detailed imitation of modern life california/ usa (in a drastical and cynical way…more or less…) even using many exact copies of real life architecture and landmarks its not far fetched that rockstar also thought about californias cultural heritage namely their native people while making the game (and presumably the mystery).

concentrating on the chumash people (haven't looked into tongva history yet unfortunately) I've done some research and came up with this (I'm not sure if i havent already read this in some thread before but i couldn't find anything by using the search function so here it is):

  • chumash people: http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chumash_people
  • painted cave: http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chumash_Painted_Cave_State_Historic_Park
  • some more info: http://www.petroglyphs.us/photographs_painted_rock_pictographs_carrizo_plain_california_PR.htm

just some random quotes to get you stoked:

  • "Remains of a developed Chumash culture, including rock paintings apparently depicting the Chumash cosmology such as Chumash Painted Cave State Historic Park can still be seen."
  • "Beyond fishing, however, they were also skilled in creating rock art. Hudson and Blackburn define rock art as "an aesthetic, symbolic representation of significant concepts and entities that is painted on or carved into a rock surface." Rock art may have been created by shamans during vision quests, most commonly in the form of pictographs (paintings on rock), but sometimes petroglyphs (engravings on rock) as well. No one is absolutely certain about the meaning of the Chumash Rock art, but scholars generally agree that it is connected with religion and astronomy."
  • "Chumash pictographs tend toward circular mandalas [compare to green light at zancudo bunker for example - a wild guess, i know…], and complex red, black and white panels [the colors of the chilliad mural!]"
  • "Paint pigments came mostly from minerals. Red was made from an iron oxide called hematite, or red ochre. White came from gypsum or diatomaceous earth. Black was made from charcoal or from manganese oxide. These ground-up pigments were mixed with a binder -- water, animal fat, or plant juices -- to make them into paint, which was applied to the rock with the fingers or with brushes made from animal tails. Chalk-like lines made with dry lumps of pigment can also be seen at some sites."
  • "Pictographs: Pictographs were painted in locations where they would be protected from the elements: in caves, alcoves, under ledges and overhangs."
  • "The Chumash considered caves, rocks, and water sources quite powerful, and the shamans saw them as a "portal to the sacred realm...where they could enter the supernatural."

further thinking and investigation:

all this made me also think again about the (momentary) location of the mural: at the top of the mountain in the cable train station? it seems out of place. i remember seeing the mural for the very first time ever, and instantly thinking something like "hmm this kind of looks like an ancient wallpainting but what is it doing in this place? isn't it supposed to be in a cave or something?". now in sound with my investigation on the chumash paintings it seems that my first thought was not that bad after all. the chilliad mural was (maybe) transferred there, (maybe) cut out from its original location (a cave or rock) and brought here as an tourist attraction.
therefore i went back to look at some known caves in the game - while remembering another early idea on the meaning of the mural which was that this mural may depict caves or tunnels in the mountain...we have all been there, but thats another story… - , so the first cave coming to mind (after the altruist cave, which i have already visited so many times before…) was the small cave located in the hills between "chumash" and the "tongva valley" (!) where a spaceship part was originally located: nothing to be seen there though (besides the spaceship part i had already picked up before, apparently...). then i went looking at the big water cave on the east coast, spaceship part and letter to be found there but again nothing else, no signs of a missing or previously cut out mural or any entry into a tunnel...

then i read more about the interpretation of the chumash paintings here: http://www.weareca.org/index.php/en/era/precontact/chumash.html. it says that the chumash believe that their first people came from santa cruz island (http://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/63/SantaCruz-Island-EO.jpg). looking at santa cruz island on google maps and looking for something similar on the gta 5 map lead me to the island east of palomino highlands cause it resembles the same shape as the real life santa cruz island quite well. guess what: nothing to be seen there… not that i expected to find something... but then eventually it all led me to a little something. something at least i haven't seen or heard of before: i stumbled across another small cave i had totally overlooked for a very long time! its a little bit north of the gta "santa cruz island" (as i call it) i had just investigated, photo and location here:

don't know if this cave has been found yet (maybe it wasn't worth mentioning cause its just empty…), but anyways this is getting way too long already, so ill just leave it by that.

tl;dr: the chumash are a native tribe based in california and they go way back (like 10.000 years). as gta5 depicts modern day california in a very detailed way and also has many places called after the chumash and tongva people in it (chumash, north chumash, tongva valley, tongva hills), it is very likely – according to my analysis of the graphical style of the mural – that the chilliad mural is a reference to the rock painting art of the chumash that can still be found at certain rocks and caves in real-life california (which are nowadays touristic landmarks). therefore i assume the author of the chilliad mural is supposed to be of native american origin, for arguments see wall of text above... . the style of the chilliad mural may not (!) look (exactly) as the style of the chumash paintings (i am totally aware of that!), but their basic formal and conceptual similarities seem to relate to each other. after further reading and investigation on the chumash paintings, i started looking at caves and islands in the game a bit and incidentally stumbled across a(n) (empty) cave on the east coast that at least i haven't encountered before. nothing more to be seen here ;)

anyways, make what you want of it – i just like how all this kind of makes sense and in the end you end up with an empty cave... thats a funny metaphor for the whole hunt imho ;) thanks for reading, happy hunting
