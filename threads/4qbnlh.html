Rehashing: Why Xs on the mural and not Check marks or numbers?

▲ 0 ptsu/saucercrabJun 28, 20167 comments at captureOpen original on Reddit ↗
Documented under:Numerology & Symbolic Number Theory ?

Just getting back into the hunt... and am still concerned with the imagery more so than anything else. The Rockstar crew are masters of design and symbolism and I feel that every visual clue connected to Chiliad was meticulously crafted.

So one thing that continues to bother me about the mural is the placement of Xs within each box, rather than check marks or numbers, or the boxes being left empty. One way of establishing a legible visual dictionary is through deduction. I like to look not at why a certain symbol was chosen, but why not.

Here are some thoughts; feel free to help me brainstorm more...

  • When do we use Xs in our culture? When do we use check marks? I feel the latter are usually used in a positive light - as a constructive achievement - while Xs (in my experience) are often used to denote the elimination, termination, or destruction of an objective. X could also "mark the spot," however, so they could be dig locations, landing locations, or similar. This leads to another coincidence supporting a theory I do not agree with, however: that the mural is a map. Admittedly, Xs are often used in coordination with maps, whereas check marks might be more commonly associated with lists.
  • But if they're marking necessary completion of items, why were the boxes not left blank, with the red Xs being filled in by the mysterious artist as as we completed the five tasks? Would empty boxes not have read as tasks? (They might have looked too much like object within the random schematic, like buildings or placeholders, or real-world boxes throughout San Andreas.) This might lend credence to the theories that these conditions must be met very quickly - if not all at once - opposed to hours or months of game play, where the player would have the ability to check the mural for updates.
  • If check marks had been used, would they have looked too abstract? Would we have thought they were arrows pointing down? Would they have looked like Vs?
  • Are the Xs representational of other in-game concepts, like Mr. X or the Xs on heist maps?
  • Why were numbers not chosen? Does this mean that the five conditions or tasks can be completed in any order, or must be met all at once?

Thanks for reading!
