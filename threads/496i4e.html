This all stems from the Aztecian "New Fire" Ceremony, which takes place at the "Hill of the Stars" upon the arrival of the Orion constellation.

A sacrificial victim has their heart cut out, then a "Firedrill" placed in their cavity that is then ignited. The flames are then spread to different parts of the land.

There also existed a secret passage to the Pyramid of the Sun on the west side, facing the sunset.

It felt all too much Chiliad Mystery when I heard about it on TV.

On TV, I think it was Grim, there was a case of a murderer putting people on crosses in various locations. It all stemmed from this ceremony, to begin a new cycle and prevent the world from ending.

Anyways, what I'm thinking is that the Altruist's are indeed required, but not in the way you're expecting.

The Ending

In the end, we're given 3 choices:

A) Kill Trevor

B) Kill Michael

These are the two important ones. These are the ones where we sacrifice one of our own.

The final option is the default option. The cracked egg. Where our ultimate reward is seeing the UFOs, as we sacrificed nothing and kept our roster full.

Now, the interesting thing about the various "Orion" symbolism we've been spotting lately, is that the Orion constellation is made up of two bright stars, and one slightly dimmer one.

Left to right we have:

Alnitak - Zeta Orionis

Alnilam - Epsilon Orionis

Mintaka - Delta Orionis

While they don't quite match up to Michael/Franklin/Trevor on the character select, you have to remember play order.

First we play Franklin, then we play Michael, and finally Trevor.

Franklin's both the first and last character we play as, he makes the choice, so he's both A and Z.

Michael is an Epsilonist, so well, hurp durp.

Trevor is the only one with a military background, and Delta is very commonly used.

Now, Alnilam, the center star, is the dimmest of the three. It's also the most sacrificial one, as Orion's shape would still persist without it. So well, Michael seems like an obvious choice.

The fact it has a dimmer star in the trio, lines up with how our character select looks after choosing option A or B. Either choices lead somewhere, but the most important one is by sacrificing Michael.

I feel this is where our rewards lie. Gordo is too mysterious not to be included in this somehow. It even has rumored alien abductions!

The mural is split into two sides. If we follow it directly, we're lead directly to the egg. If we follow the clues on either side, we should be lead to the rewards.

3 is actually used quite a few times in our mystery, so that's another reason why I feel the sacrificial number of 3 is important.

Final

Now, we know time is important to a few of the secrets. The sun rises on the east, and sets on the west.

So, Sunrise for Yoga, and Sunset for Altruist Camp.

Or our usual time of 3am.

The lightning doesn't represent electricity, it represents power. Godly power. ( See the Aztec Pyramid? ;) )

As in the mural is a message from the gods (Rockstar). As are its rewards.

The most widely known fact about the Aztecs, is the fact they did ritual sacrifices. Specific Sacrifices for Specific Reasons.

They also built their temples and pyramids according to the suns position, which was their main deity, Tonatiuh.

They would sacrifice people because they feared the sun wouldn't move if they didn't. So, it was a way of them to... "Give Thanks"

See why sacrificing only 3 people might be important? We know they want 4 sacrifices, but we always end up getting a shootout if we bring a forth or fifth to the camp. Nevermind the fact that their final sacrifice is set to be Trevor (not the people you brought last), who then kills them before they can complete it, thus breaking the ceremony.

So, instead of having the altruists attempt to sacrifice Trevor, which we know never works, lets complete the ceremony ourselves and the 4th sacrifice required, be it Michael or Trevor depending on the reward we seek.

It's also very fitting Trevor dies by fire, and that Michael joins in on the kill of the one who considers him his bestfriend, effectively breaking Trevor's heart prior to his death.
