A considered comparison of the the tattoo map with the SA map.

Documented under:Mural & Map Mirroring ?

Well, I had a go at overlaying the two maps: https://imgur.com/9BNngYD.jpg

Not perfect but oh well. I've aggressively thresholded the tattoo map as it had a grey tint, which is why the edges look weird. Thick black areas are the shallow bays and coves on the original map.

I personally consider both of these to be viable maps of the same location. I've considered the two maps from the perspective of analyzing genuine historical maps in the context of a modern, real-world one. The relationship between these two fictional maps is, at times, surprisingly similar to what one would expect if this were really a comparison of a 300+ year old map and a current one. Red lines match up what are to me, the same geographic features on each of the two maps. Of note is that several of the more important features are present and crudely replicated. There are, of course, three major differences which I have numbered, but I believe these have a possible explanation:

  1. The spur that houses the end of the Zancudo runway. Looking at this in game, it's pretty clear that it's been artificially raised to construct the runway, it's not a naturally flat landmass. In fact, the appearance of the beach actually is a very good representation of artificially-reclaimed land. Therefore, it wouldn't feature on an old map, since it was built for no other purpose than to contain the runway.
  2. LSIA and the Port of LS. Both of these are, if you look in game, built primarily on dockyard or other concrete structures. They're not natural, it's a complete artificial harbor. Everything south of La Puerta is built this way. LSIA's edges have a lot of reinforcement and shoring up. I'm prepared to say that everything in this area is relatively recent, and so is the ground it's built on. It's quite possible that before Los Santos was built, this was a shallow, beachy cove all the way around.
  3. The north-east shore. Actually, the shape here isn't too bad, but the scale is pretty off. The same can be said for the rest of the map and this is consistent with real-world early cartography, although feature description was easy, accurately gauging scale was not.

Other features I've noticed concern the water. The lake that's present is clearly the Alamo Sea, although again the scale is poor. Of particular note to me is that in current SA, the other major lakes, Lake Vinewood and the Land Act Reservoir, are both artificial. Naturally, therefore, they don't feature on the early map. The pipeline up to Land Act and the East LS river are also both heavily altered from their natural courses. So, the river heading south on the map matches up initially with the river leading to Lago Zancudo, then heads along the path of that within Great Chaparral, uphill (relevant?) to where Lake Vinewood, which is artificially dammed, is located. It then also seems to approximate the path of the river up to the Land Act Reservoir (underground in pipes) and out into East LS. For me, this river is a pretty good match.

All in all, there's an awful lot that for me, provides evidence that the tattoo map is indeed San Andreas.
