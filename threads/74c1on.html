Rockstar has given us many clues that allude to the fact that the Altruist Cult is secretly a sub-sect of the vastly powerful Epsilon Program.

The Altruists are an isolated cult of baby-boomers that perform a strange sacrificial ritual on the victims that are brought to their camp in the mountains.

There are many clues alluding to their involvement in the Epsilon Program, including a subliminal message on Vinewood Blvd. Radio. The host suggests that the co-host should join the Altruist Cult...and then says that he would look great in "baby blue", the official color of Epsilon.

There is another subtle clue on the wooden sign in Raton Canyon near the docks, which is a map of the surrounding area.

A hidden message is carved into this map that says “Actuality”.

Alongside it is another carving of a “Fort” drawn on the map right where the Altruist Camp is located, with an arrow pointing to it. This clue alludes to the fact that the experience of “Self-Actualization” can be achieved at the Altruist Camp.

Achieving “Self-Actualization” is the ultimate goal of the Epsilon Program...to transform yourself into an immortal alien-God and realize your true self. This transformation is achieved by adhering to Upward Generosity, the ultimate moral code of the Program.

Upward Generosity is described in the Tract as the giving of everything that you have to the Epsilonists that are upwards of you in the hierarchy. In order to fully achieve this... followers must be taken to the Altruists to endure their sacrificial ritual.

This sacrifice is described as a gift to Lord Zapho, one of the rulers of the immortal reptilian alien race that the Altruists and Epsilonists believe they belong to.

The details of this strange ritual are unknown...however, it is implied to be some sort of punishment in which the Altruists drug you with powerful psychedelics and cannibalize you.

Enduring this punishment is seen a “right of passage” into joining the Cult.

The intense psychedelics that victims are dosed with often cause them to believe that they have transformed themselves into an immortal alien-God...finding their true self.

This ultimate transition into a God is described within the metaphors of the Tract, which uses the metaphors of different forms of Birds and Plants to describe different kinds of people.

Trees are used as metaphors for female Epsilonists and the Thorn Bush and Cactus are used as a metaphor for female doubters.

Fruit is used as a metaphor to describe a young person who may one day become an Epsilonist. The offspring of a Tree. The Thorn Bush and Cactus bear no fruit.

Ch.1 Vrs. 1 of the Tract states that “an apple sometimes flies up from a tree and becomes a great Eagle”...and describes this as “the most selfless act of all”.

The Eagle is used as a metaphor to describe the immortal God Form that Epsilonists believe they are transforming into.

This roughly translates to... “an Epsilonist's child is taken away from its mother and is given to those upwards of him to be transformed into a God”.

This is where the “Altruists” get their name.

Even though they are performing a horrific cannibalistic ritual on their victims, they believe that they are doing a selfless act...since their victims will soon after transform into a God. This is why the Tract describes this as the “most selfless act of all”.

Making this transformation is the only means of saving your soul from the upcoming Apocalypse. Doubters in this are “Unsaveable”.

This is why Ron Jakowski tells Trevor that the Altruists are looking for “lost souls who need to be saved”.

The Epsilon Program gains new members by abducting hitchhikers and taking them to the Altruist Camp to be cannibalized and introduced to their mind controlling drugs. Due to the Epsilonist's belief that they are all alien-Gods....victims often leave this psychedelic experience believing that they had been abducted by aliens.

This is the explanation behind all of the ‘missing hitchhiker’ reports on the radio throughout the main story.

This is what happened to Omega.

The Epsilon Program lures people to be abducted with their online Identity Evaluation…luring people to Raton Canyon, right by the Altruist Camp. This is what happens to Michael during “Seeking the Truth”.

Cris Formage also has an All-Seeing Eye on San Andreas (the Hippie Camp UFO), and often has Epsilonists go and abduct people when they pass out. This is what happens to Michael during “Did Somebody Say Yoga?”…he believes that he is being abducted by aliens when in reality he has been abducted by the Epsilon Program.

Trevor also gets abducted every time he huffs gas at his trailer. If you huff gas in 1st Person Trevor will pass out with his hand right in front of the screen, revealing his All-Seeing Eye tattoo…alluding to the fact that Cris sees all things. After being abducted, the Epsilonists usually drop T off in an area that has some reference to aliens.

Sometimes after switching characters, Trevor will wake up in a dress atop Mt. Gordo and say “A less rational man might’ve thought he’d been abducted by aliens!”

If Trevor decides to abduct people and bring them to the Camp himself...he is essentially doing the work of the Epsilon Program...sending people to be eaten by our reptilian alien overlords.

All of the alien references and agricultural metaphors within the Epsilon Tract are a direct reference to the Rockstar game “Body Harvest”.

This iconic title is an open world sandbox game that was released on the N64 in 1998.

The game takes place in the then futuristic year of 2016, after nearly all of mankind has been harvested by an alien race for their organic material. Players take the role of Adam Drake, one of the last survivors who is sent back in time to stop the aliens from harvesting mankind into extinction.

This game uses the metaphor “harvest” to describe the consumption of humans by this invading alien race...comparing them to the crops of the field.

The agricultural metaphors within the Epsilon Tract are used in the same exact way.

The Epsilon Tract commands the reader to have lots of sex with other Epsilonists (trees) so that the offspring may one day be “harvested” by the Altruists.

Ch. 2 Vrs. 2 tells the reader to “Hold on to the peach tree and the apple tree and give all that you have in faith and watch fruits flow upward to a bountiful harvest.”

Ch. 3 Vrs. 3 states that “If a woman does not lie with five upward men of Epsilon on the seventh month, the harvest will surely be meager.”

Ch. 3 Vrs. 5 of the Tract states that “Just as a fool who saves his fruit sees it rot, a wise man sends his fruit towards the upwards.”

This is a reference to the belief that being harvested by the Altruists will give you immortality...and unless you do this you will “rot” with age.

In GTA: San Andreas, the Epsilon Program is secretly introduced into the storyline when CJ first meets The Truth....during a mission called “Body Harvest”.

Truth asks Carl to steal a harvester from a farm near Mt. Chiliad. He explains, “I’m a man of peace, but, some squares across the ridge are not respecting my peace. I mean, survivalist maniacs! Right-wingers! Fascists! They have a harvester and I need one!”

The Truth never explains to Carl who these people are, but there are several clues that allude to the fact that they are Epsilonists. The farm itself is labeled “Cult Location” on the map of San Andreas and it has been discovered that all of the windows strangely light up baby blue at night…unlike every other window in San Andreas, which lights up yellow.

Also, many of the Tractors that appear around this farm are baby blue, the color of the Epsilon Program.

This mission serves as a reference to the fact that Epsilonists believe to be an alien race.

During this mission, CJ switches roles with these alleged aliens and harvests them with the giant Combine Harvester that he steals from their farm.

All of these agricultural metaphors that are used by the Epsilon Program are nothing less than a subtle reference to the game “Body Harvest”, which is essentially a sci-fi version of Grand Theft Auto.

This is why Michael receives a Tractor if he chooses to stay loyal to the Epsilon Program...an agricultural machine that is often used to pull a Harvester.
