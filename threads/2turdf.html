From Wikipedia: an instrument attached to a telescope to analyze the radiation from astronomical objects. It uses a prism or a grating to spread the light from the object into a spectrum, a rainbow of colors.

From this we get the color spectrum and also the acronym Roy G. Biv. Then of course the color wheel that represents an endless cycle of color, for they bleed into one another endlessly.

The painted glyphs found at the camp itself contain most of these colors and also a sun. Each glyph has a matching message as well.

Both color and the sun have a cycle.

The same cannot be said about the red messages and their proper position is debatable.

Everything else can be placed into the spectrum accordingly, with the largest glyph (yellow/orange) being the center of it all. Painted on the water tower near the entrance of the camp, facing the sunrise which appears between the hill above the camp and Mt. Chiliad. A gorgeous view of the sunset can later be seen from the cliff by the sacrificial stone.
