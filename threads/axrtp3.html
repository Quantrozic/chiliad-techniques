Uranus is the 7th planet in the Solar System by its distance from the Sun, the 3rd by size and the 4th by mass. It owes its name to the Roman god of the sky, Uranus. Uranus is the first planet discovered in modern times. Uranus is a giant planet, and more precisely a giant ice planet. Like other gas giants, Uranus has a system of rings, a magnetosphere and many natural satellites. There are 27 satellites and 13 narrow rings.

The rings are named, in order of increasing distance: Zeta, 6, 5, 4, Alpha, Beta, Eta, Gamma, Delta, Lambda, Epsilon, Nu and Mu.

Epsilon is the densest, outer and brightest ring of the Uranus rings. It is the one for which two shepherd satellites could be clearly identified: Cordelia, inside, and Ophelia, outside. The position of Cordelia and Ophelia makes it possible to stabilize the Epsilon ring of Uranus by delimiting its edges. The Epsilon ring was discovered by accident on March 10, 1977.

Cordelia and Ophelia are located within the radius of the synchronous orbit; that is, their orbits are so low that they take less time to complete the complete turn of Uranus, than Uranus takes to do a complete turn on itself. The tidal forces exerted by Uranus therefore slowly slow down Cordelia and Ophelia, which lose altitude and will eventually crash into Uranus one day. These are two fairly similar satellites.
