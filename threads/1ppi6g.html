Zen and the art of jetpack hunting

▲ 21 ptsu/lendotNov 1, 201312 comments at captureOpen original on Reddit ↗
Documented under:Hidden Map Art & Environmental Pattern Recognition

I have a confession to make. I stopped looking for jetpacks and UFOs and all that some time ago. This may sound very counter-intuitive, but I feel this is the best possible contribution I can make to this quest at this time. No, I haven't given up. I've simply taken a different philosophical approach at the most fundamental of levels--if the jetpack exists it will find me, so to speak, not the other way around. This is but one of many different approaches that I think need to be undertaken concurrently by the community at large, so it's just another niche. But if you find yourself as I did, turning into Fox Mulder and teetering on frustration and insanity, this may be a change of pace worth considering.

What I'm doing is roaming around with one goal and one goal only: taking in the sights and sounds. I'm living in the moment and simply appreciating all the hard work and detail that went into the world design and trying to immerse myself in that as much as possible. I'm not looking for anything; words like "jetpack" aren't even in my game vocabulary anymore. Instead, I let things reveal themselves to me. An obscured easter egg here, an unexpected building interior there, a random sign telling me to go visit such-and-such street on the other side of town for no apparent reason, a curiously bizarre and cryptic sign outside a restaurant...you get the idea. I take a photo and move on. Later I look over my gallery and ponder the things I've encountered. Clues? Maybe. To what? I don't know. I just try to stay in the moment, take things at face value and let them lead me where they may, and not get too obsessed over any one thing.

Right now I have 2-3 things I'm investigating that are pretty far off the beaten path of where other explorations are focused. Even if they lead nowhere, or to something less exciting than a jetpack, I'm having fun doing what I'm doing and if nothing else, I can at least contribute to the collective game knowledge in the form of easter eggs and whatnot that I've found along the way. All I know is, this approach is opening my eyes to a ton of little things I never in a million years would have picked up on if I were going out there primed to look for jetpack clues, and that's keeping me open to the possibility that one or more of these could lead somewhere, wherever that may be. Sounds strange, but I'm actually keeping the clue space much more finite and manageable this way while at the same time finding many more interesting things than I would otherwise. I believe therein lies the value, both in the broader search for potential clues and in my own experience with the game.

Anyway, I figured I'd share that in case it resonates with any of you who find yourselves running into brick wall after brick wall and are looking for a change.
