Next step at the camp & karma with the Altruists!

Hey guys. Me here again. I'm starting to really like digging deeper into this mystery. Testing things out and giving/taking information to/from this place is just a really fun experience for me, and I enjoy being a part of this community.

With that said, I'm here with two other theories that I felt I could squeeze together into one post. The first theory is what we have to do after the Altruists shoot-out. If you didn't know, after the shoot-out text that reads "Altruists cleared" appears on your screen. After that text vanishes, you cannot quick-save the game. This error only happens when doing missions, Strangers & Freaks, random events, pretty much anything that requires you to do a certain task while playing. When you leave the Altruists camp, you can finally quick-save your game as normal. This proves that after the shoot-out, we are still doing a random event, but we do not know what our next step is as the game does not give us any steps on-screen.

What do I think we have to do? Well, here's where my theory comes in. So Rockstar most likely expected us to do most of the random events after 100%, but this is just pure speculation from my part. (I only think that because 16 random events are all that we need for 100%, and there actually way more than that in the game.) But if you choose to leave Trevor alive after the final mission, we get a very strange Strangers & Freaks mission involving Trevor seeing his mother. After finishing the mission, Trevor returns to his trailer only to find his mother left/vanished. He then becomes really depressed and drunk. During a storyline mission where he is driving Patricia home, he cries to her: "Everybody I love abandons me." His mother, Michael, Michael's family, Patricia, and some more we don't meet in-game. But as we can tell from the context the game gives us, Trevor is really close friends with the Altruists. He's not in it for the money (well, he kinda is but whatever), he really is an Altruist. Notice how the members of the camp say "it's a sign" when Trevor delivers a hitchhiker. You know how good this probably makes Trevor feel? He thinks they worship him & love him.

Well, he's wrong. Turns out, the camp members turn on him and Trevor is forced to kill them all. Now where am I getting at with this? Well, Trevor will probably realize all of the faults he has made, with one of them being that he delivered those innocent people to the Altruists. So I think we have to free them. We deliver five people, and how many houses in the camp stay active even after we clear the Altruists? Five. I think the five hitchhikers are staying in these houses, but are afraid to leave because of the Altruists. How we free them, I don't know. I need some help from the community for that answer. Now I know there is nothing in the game files supporting this theory, but after we find out how to free these people, a cutscene can play that shows us helping them. Nobody even has to have new dialogue for this cutscene after all!

There's also that phrase around the camp "our path is lit." I know they worship the sun and all, but I noticed something at the camp that can really only be tested with after you clear the members. Certain lights at the camp infinitely respawn after being shot out or broken in anyway. These lights are mainly on the houses near the camp entrance. We may need to use these lights as a path of some sort (either in the camp or elsewhere) or shoot them out in a specific order. Perhaps this "path" is one of the many paths on the mural we need? XD

Alright, after that far-fetched theory, here's my second and more believable one. You know all that talk about karma? And that we need to make our own karma in the game? Well, what if I told you that we already get karma in the game...! There is a couple of karma moments that stand out, but here's the one I wanna talk about. So Trevor delivers random people to the Altruists for money and because he is a member of their cult. Well, what happens to Trevor for doing this? He gets himself nearly killed! And he has to kill his own religious brothers! That's karma if you ask me. So if we do need good karma, perhaps not delivering anybody to the camp is the key? Or are we actually supposed to be looking at the Altruists' karma? In this case, they send out Trevor to deliver innocent people, and then they pay him back by sacrificing him! But what happens to those bastards? Trevor retaliates, gets out a gun, and shoots the hell out of them. That's worse karma than Trevor's if you ask me.

But anyways, what do you guys think of this? Am I wearing too big of a tinfoil hat? Or am I onto something? Leave your suggestions and feedback below! KIFFLOM! XD
