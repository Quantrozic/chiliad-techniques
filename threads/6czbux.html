A lot of subtle weirdness at the Betsy O'Neil Pavilion. Strange music, Metric road signs, light refraction caused by heat, cryptic banners, etc.

I think mystery hunters should take a look at this performing arts center, located in the southwest corner of I-2 and I-5.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5kIY4OcfD48

When you walk up to the walls on the first floor, you can hear disconcerting symphonic music playing, similar in the way you hear music coming from the Epsilon building.

Underneath the building, there is a street and parking lot. The same wave motion that happens when a character gets drunk will happen here. It appears to be coming from the vents in the walls. Hard to see in photo or video. Go there in person. This doesn't happen anywhere else in the game.

http://i.imgur.com/BjdLlfE.jpg

Another curious thing to note about this tunnel are the non-US street signs. A sign that reads 10 km rather than 5 mph. And "No Entry" signs typically seen in Europe.

http://i.imgur.com/JZN1C4I.jpg

The above head fluorescent lighting in the parking lot is brighter than any other light source in the game.

http://i.imgur.com/PrMrr9W.jpg

The banners on the building itself are odd. They don't satirize anything. They're not sexual puns. They contain random phrases and images that turn up interesting results when googled.

Quid Novi? Six: which translates from Latin to English as "What's new? Six." Well, what comes after GTA V? Also, can anyone identify the individual in the banner?

Castello Monte - Translates from Italian to English as "Castle Mountain". Also, a rural location in Northern Italy. A WWII battle was fought here in 1944. Coincidentally but probably unrelated, the number 1944 appears on the base of two nearby billboards.

A Knight on the Tiles - Possibly a reference to Dr. Roger Penrose's Tiling problem. The Tiling problem posits that it is impossible to create an algorithm to tile a plane with a given set of polygons. This could also be a reference to the game of chess, based on the image.

Chorale Miasma - Chorale is defined as "a musical composition (or part of one) consisting of or resembling a harmonized version of a simple, stately hymn tune." Miasma is defined as "an oppressive or unpleasant atmosphere that surrounds or emanates from something." Consider both the music from the building and the air underneath. Unrelated, but chorale is an anagram of the word cholera. It was thought in the middle ages that cholera was spread by a miasma.

"Rite of Passage" by Alexzander Gerschon. The name "Alexzander" of Greek origin, means "defender of the people". Gershon was a character in the Bible, son of Levi. Gershon's descendants the Gerschonites, were tasked with taking care of the curtains of the Temple.

Symphony in A Minor - Notice the ballerina is holding a goblet. Also note how in "Chorale Miasma", the image of half woman/half goblet

One more thing going back to the 10 km sign. The arrows, which guide the flow of traffic, all lead to a arrow pointing north. If you head 10 km north from that spot, you'll end up near the base of Mt. Chiliad, at a point where the mountain fits into the shape on the mural.

http://i.imgur.com/BVDydWp.png

Go to the spot that lines up with the jetpack symbol and you end up in a tunnel with two vault doors with keypads, T01 and T02. But I'm sure everyone here knows about these doors.

Maybe all of this is random. My theory is that a lot these are multiple Easter eggs and references unrelated to each other.
