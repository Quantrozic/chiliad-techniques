Killing Trevor and the Karma Theory

▲ 6 ptsu/Brother-BrotherFeb 18, 201441 comments at captureOpen original on Reddit ↗
Documented under:Karma / Pacifist Playthrough Protocol

As we play through the game, more and more clues begin to show up as to what happened that fateful day in North Yankton. The dialogue of Michael and Dave Norton clearly state that Trevor was supposed to be killed, but instead Brad obviously died and Michael was shot. Therefore the whole intention of Michael turning everybody in was so that they could get rid of Trevor, and Brad and Michael could make it out safe.

Now we know that two of the UFO's contain writing pertaining to the FIB. Those two being the one on Chiliad and above the Hippy Camp.

So possibly for the final mission in which Franklin has to choose who lives and who dies, they must kill Trevor for FIB. Potentially yielding a reward from the FIB for doing what they never could. Also Trevor is clearly unsavable and has the ability to literally do anything that he wants. Now obviously nothing happens if you were to choose this ending under normal circumstances, but this where Karma comes into play.

This is where my theory becomes tricky, and tons of factors start to pile up. First off I'll start with the Karma part. As you play through the game, there will be tons of blue and red dots that you get the option to kill. Now whenever you get to these points, choose Trevor to kill them, otherwise let them live. Because we all know that he is a murdering psychopath. He's also the only character who kills anybody when you are first introduced to the characters. Michael never has to shoot anybody in North Yankton and all conflict can be resolved by playing as Trevor. But this doesn't include people who are already trying to kills you such as Merryweather, NOOSE, Ballas, and the police during missions. But getting stars outside of missions and killing the police may count as innocents. Therefore, playing through the game, and not killing anybody as Michael or Franklin may bring the reward in the end. However it may not because the second biggest factor comes into play is, that do you need to get 100% before you kill Trevor? By this I mean accomplishing everything Trevor can do, then doing the mission with Franklin to kill him.

Personally I don't think you do. But nobody can say for sure because this hasn't been tested. With the textile mural it states that its how you play the game, and not that you won.

I'm going to begin testing this theory probably next week. I need to take a break since I just completed my second playthrough. Any thoughts or things to add please leave a comment.
