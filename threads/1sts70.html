Something I Had Overlooked Regarding the "Mountain" Glyph and Area 51 Files

Documented under:Edge-Detection Glyph Tracing ?

Hey all, I'm very much of the opinion that the glyph called the "mountain" glyph has more of a meaning than we attribute to it, and seeing a couple posts recently has made me think of some new ideas, though I have to admit, the only reasonable connection I can make is the mural itself and the way a secret mountain interior could work.

Someone posted in a thread a while back that the mountain glyph could be referencing a winding mountain path because the zig-zag lines do not fully extend to the sides of the glyph. I had noticed that before, and I had also noticed when I first saw the glyph that the left-most side is very faint, or illegible.

Secondly, I've always thought that the mural represented an interior cavern of sorts, mostly for the fact that the lines near the top-left portion are somewhat redundant. (some connect to others more than once, for example)

I looked again at the "mountain" glyph. I thought, perhaps it is referencing WHICH mountain. That was where I stuck, hoping we'd find more clues. Then I thought about it more after seeing a video of a man trying to glitch toward a spiral staircase. It hit me...

Could the glyph be referencing an internal spiral staircase? If you look at the mural, it has a straight line going all the way down to the "easter egg" which connects to every other portion. This sounds pretty crazy, but then again if there was an elevator for this portion, it would be pretty easy to find in the files I believe. I'm a little iffy on the idea of an interior for Chiliad considering we haven't found files for it, but I don't expect a huge, detailed interior, probably a simple one. This makes sense to me because we have files for an "area_51" which to my knowledge, could be the interior we're looking for. There is supposedly a large military base at Groom Lake that is underground near a mountain. Some even believe the base extends to a nearby mountain. (that's the kicker)

Someone else was adamant that the symbol next to the sand glyph was intended to mean "water" or "lake" which makes a good impression on the idea that this base is meant to parallel "Groom Lake"

Here are some fun quips from Wikipedia's page on Area 51:

  • "Many of the hypotheses concern underground facilities at Groom or at Papoose Lake (also known as "S-4 location"), 8.5 miles (13.7 km) south, and include claims of a transcontinental underground railroad system, a disappearing airstrip (nicknamed the "Cheshire Airstrip", after Lewis Carroll's Cheshire cat) which briefly appears when water is sprayed onto its camouflaged asphalt, and engineering based on alien technology. Publicly available satellite imagery, however, reveals clearly visible landing strips at Groom Dry Lake, but not at Papoose Lake"
  • "The shape of OXCART was unprecedented, with its wide, disk-like fuselage designed to carry vast quantities of fuel"

These are both honestly merely interesting trivia.

However, it makes a good amount of sense that there would be another secret base besides the Fort Zancudo. All of this really depends on the base that Fort Zancudo is supposed to emulate, if Zancudo is the exterior base many see as "Groom Lake" and there is an interior, the railway would go from under Zancudo to Chiliad, the true "Area 51." This also can explain the idea of tunnels underground to the FIB and IAA headquarters. We have a train system in this game as well, an extensive network at that.

The staircase idea is more fit to describe how one would find themselves inside the mountain, unless those vertical lines drawn in the mural are elevators or ladders instead of a staircase. I do not think this is the case, considering some of the lines on the mural are horizontal, and some are at acute angles.

I need to look at the files we found for "Area 51" in the game, but I think there were destructible shutters (Probably to enter/exit the facility, maybe at where the lines touch the outside of the "mountain")
