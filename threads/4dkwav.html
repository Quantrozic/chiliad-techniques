Maze Bank Circle Fountain - Grey Code?

▲ 32 ptsu/walkeronlineApr 6, 201624 comments at captureOpen original on Reddit ↗
Documented under:Base-N and ASCII Re-encoding Sweeps

Here is the album as usual!

So, let me start by saying this was an entirely random discovery. I was searching for ways things could be encoded into circles and stumbled upon something. Decided to go in game and try to map out the Circle Fountain at the Maze Bank. Here is what i found.

  1. The fountain can be split into 32 parts, consisting of 4 sections each. The split is done at the lights that are inside the fountain because one of the lights is perfect due north. What this means is the fountain can be split into 8 4-bit sections based on cardinal directions.
  2. After using bullets to segment off each part of each line going around the fountain, i noticed that the outer lines are NOT perfect circles...instead they seem to be pieces of a smaller circle, and actually i believe could be pieces of the original 32 sections in the middle circle. This is based on the angles at which some of the pieces are congruent to the fountain. If you compare the arch of the outer pieces, its clear they were not meant to form another circle AWAY from the main one, but one track with multiple layers. So instead of thinking multiple tracks like a record, think of multiple bit stacking ONTO the origional 32 section circle, like stacking blocks.
  3. The main fountain has 32 sections, but there is 34 sections in total around the fountain. The outer single pieces seem to be the oddity at this point.
  4. Looking once again at the bits around the fountain, you will notice that one section has a gap that is smaller than one total bit. I do not know how to line this gap up properly.

So, why do i mention all this? Because i just stumbled across Gray Code.

Gray code, from what i can tell, is like a form of binary that works by only changing one digit at a time, unlike binary that changes 2 at a time.

The Wikipedia covers this pretty well

Interestingly enough, we also have Rotary encoding and Single-Track Gray code, which would actually fit with the fountain in multiple ways.

If its rotary, we need only line up the outside fountain segments with their interior ones and count them outward to get our code.
If its Single-Track encoding, we need to find the points where it calculates its number (I'm speculating the outer single sections around the fountain) and rotate the code around until we end up with the sequence.

Googling Gray Code gives you a LOT of resources, as well as Single Track Gray Code.

So what do we do from here?

We need to match the angles of the outer pieces to the 32 fountain pieces to determine where they sit. If you look for yourself, you will notice that the segments don't line up perfectly \(Picture 11 shows this perfectly\) But some of them do match the ANGLES of pieces in the 32 segment ring of the fountain. You can see me trying to line up some of them in the last pictures with Franklin. I'm basically matching them to their closest angular segment and assigning them sections. I broke each one down by 4 bits, with letters for each 4-bit section, giving me A1 to A4, B1 to B4, and so on to H1-H4

However, using my method of Bullet Marking, i actually reached the decal limit and had to stop.

How you can help

File scrapers - We need the mesh from the Maze Bank brought into a 3d modeling program to check the angles of the fountain segments against the fountain itself to find their true positions.

In Game - You can eyeball the angles of the pieces to determine their order. I will say using my Bullet Method to label the segments works but has a limit eventually, so the process could be simplified.

If anyone knows about gray code (Its a bit over my head at this point) They should look into how the lights and fountain segments can be arranged into such a thing.

I have a small inkling of an idea to try once we figure the code out, but I'll save that for more speculation down the road.
